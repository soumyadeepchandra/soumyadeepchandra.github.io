I am a Ph.D. candidate in Electrical and Computer Engineering at Purdue University, advised by <a href='https://engineering.purdue.edu/NRL/Group'>Prof. Kaushik Roy</a> in the Nanoelectronics Research Laboratory. I received my B.E. (Hons.) in Electronics and Telecommunication Engineering from Jadavpur University, India.

My research focuses on **semiconductor thermal modeling, 3D heterogeneous integration (3DHI), advanced packaging, and AI for hardware**. I build high-fidelity **COMSOL finite-element simulation** workflows and **physics-informed surrogate models** (neural operators and generative networks) that predict steady-state and transient chip temperatures orders of magnitude faster than FEM, enabling rapid evaluation of chip, power-map and package configurations early in the design cycle.

Recent work includes **Therm3D**, a physics-aware neural operator for transient thermal modeling of 3DHI systems (1.09 °C RMSE, ~10,000–20,000× faster than FEM), and **2D-ThermAl** (IEEE TCAD), a physics-informed generative framework for circuit-level thermal analysis (0.71 °C RMSE, ~200× faster than COMSOL). Earlier in my Ph.D., I worked on video and scene understanding, including optimal-transport methods for procedure learning (<a href='https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/poster/93812'>OPEL, NeurIPS 2024</a>) and surgical video analysis.

My background spans **VLSI design, circuit power and thermal analysis, and physics-informed machine learning**. I am a finalist for the 2026 Qualcomm Innovation Fellowship and an ICML 2026 Golden Reviewer.

**I expect to graduate in Spring 2027** and am open to full-time opportunities in thermal modeling, advanced packaging, and AI-driven hardware design.
