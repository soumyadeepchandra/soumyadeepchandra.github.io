About

Finding the steps hidden in video.

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Electrical and Computer Engineering at Purdue University, advised by Prof. Kaushik Roy in the Nanoelectronics Research Laboratory. I received my B.E. (Hons.) in Electronics and Telecommunication Engineering from Jadavpur University, India.

My video research focuses on learning structure from unaligned and weakly labeled video. I develop optimal-transport methods that discover how steps line up across demonstrations, even when steps are missing, reordered or filmed from different viewpoints.

  • OPEL (NeurIPS 2024): optimal-transport-guided procedure learning across first- and third-person video.
  • REMAP: regularized partial Gromov–Wasserstein optimal transport for weakly supervised procedure learning, robust to missing, reordered or unmatched steps.
  • ViPeR (IEEE Access 2025): surgical phase recognition that captures temporal structure and action-level information from video.
  • Loss Knows Best: detecting mislabeled frames in video datasets from model-loss trajectories.
  • Image Grammar (IEEE Access 2024): structured reasoning over the compositional and syntactic relationships among objects in a scene.

Alongside this, I build physics-informed models for chip thermal analysis (Therm3D, and 2D-ThermAl in IEEE TCAD). That work gives me hands-on experience with spatiotemporal modeling, large simulated datasets and models that must respect physical constraints.

I am a finalist for the 2026 Qualcomm Innovation Fellowship, an ICML 2026 Golden Reviewer, and a reviewer for NeurIPS, ICLR, ICML, WACV and MICCAI. I graduate in Spring 2027.
