About

Modeling heat, at the speed of design.

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Electrical and Computer Engineering at Purdue University, advised by Prof. Kaushik Roy in the Nanoelectronics Research Laboratory. I received my B.E. (Hons.) in Electronics and Telecommunication Engineering from Jadavpur University, India.

My research focuses on semiconductor thermal modeling, 3D heterogeneous integration (3DHI), advanced packaging, and AI for hardware. I build high-fidelity COMSOL finite-element simulation workflows and physics-informed surrogate models (neural operators and generative networks) that predict steady-state and transient chip temperatures orders of magnitude faster than FEM, enabling rapid evaluation of chip, power-map and package configurations early in the design cycle.

Recent work includes Therm3D, a physics-aware neural operator for transient thermal modeling of 3DHI systems (1.09 °C RMSE, ~10,000–20,000× faster than FEM), and 2D-ThermAl (IEEE TCAD), a physics-informed generative framework for circuit-level thermal analysis (0.71 °C RMSE, ~200× faster than COMSOL). Earlier in my Ph.D., I worked on video and scene understanding, including optimal-transport methods for procedure learning (OPEL, NeurIPS 2024) and surgical video analysis.

My background spans VLSI design, circuit power and thermal analysis, and physics-informed machine learning. I am a finalist for the 2026 Qualcomm Innovation Fellowship and an ICML 2026 Golden Reviewer.

I expect to graduate in Spring 2027 and am open to full-time opportunities in thermal modeling, advanced packaging, and AI-driven hardware design. Feel free to reach out by email or on LinkedIn.

    Therm3D: Physics-Aware Neural Operator for Transient Thermal Modeling in 3D Heterogeneous Integration Systems

    Soumyadeep Chandra, Sayeed Shafayet Chowdhury, and Kaushik Roy

    Under review at Design, Automation and Test in Europe (DATE)

    A physics-informed neural operator framework for near-real-time steady-state and transient thermal analysis of 3D heterogeneous integration (3DHI) systems. An automated COMSOL–Python FEM pipeline generates synthetic training data, and a point-cloud latent neural operator with coordinate-conditioned decoding and multi-stage autoregressive rollout performs long-horizon 3D thermal prediction. Energy conservation, boundary-condition enforcement and source/hotspot-aware modeling improve physical consistency. Therm3D achieves 1.09 °C RMSE on unseen power maps over transient trajectories while running roughly 10,000–20,000x faster than FEM, enabling rapid evaluation of 3DHI power and package configurations.

  2. IEEE TCAD
    IEEE TCAD2025Selected

    2D-ThermAl: Physics-Informed Framework for Thermal Analysis of Circuits using Generative AI

    Soumyadeep Chandra, Sayeed Shafayet Chowdhury, and Kaushik Roy

    IEEE Transactions on Computer-Aided Design of Integrated Circuits and Systems (TCAD)

    arXivCode

    Thermal analysis is increasingly critical in modern integrated circuits, where non-uniform power dissipation and high transistor densities can cause rapid temperature spikes and reliability concerns. ThermAl is a physics-informed generative AI framework that identifies heat sources and estimates full-chip transient and steady-state thermal distributions directly from input activity profiles. It uses a hybrid U-Net with positional encoding and a physics-aware regularizer, trained on a dataset of 200+ COMSOL-simulated circuit configurations ranging from logic gates to complex designs. ThermAl achieves 0.71 °C RMSE and runs up to  200x faster than conventional FEM tools, with full-scale error below 2.2% across a 25–95 °C operating range.

    ViPeR: Vision-Based Surgical Phase Recognition

    Soumyadeep Chandra, Sayeed Shafayet Chowdhury, Courtney Yong, Ginnie Jeng, Ashorne Mahenthiran, Kostantinos E. Morris, Harrison L. Love, Chandru P. Sundaram, and Kaushik Roy

    IEEE Access

    PaperCode

    Surgical phase recognition is a critical yet challenging problem, hindered by the scarcity of well-annotated medical datasets and the substantial inter- and intra-procedural variation of surgical workflows. ViPeR is a vision-based model for phase recognition that achieves state-of-the-art performance of 91.7% on Cholec80 and 66.4% on UroSlice, a more challenging dataset with irregular phase durations, non-standardized phase order, and a smaller sample size.

  6. NeurIPS
    NeurIPS2024Selected

    OPEL: Optimal Transport Guided ProcedurE Learning

    Sayeed Shafayet Chowdhury, Soumyadeep Chandra, and Kaushik Roy

    Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS)

    PaperDOISlides

    Procedure learning is the task of identifying the key-steps of a task and determining their logical order, given several videos of the same task. OPEL treats video frames as samples from an unknown distribution and frames their distance calculation as an optimal transport problem, relaxing the frame-to-frame and monotonic-alignment assumptions of prior work. Optimality and temporal priors regularize the transport plan. OPEL significantly outperforms the state of the art on both egocentric and third-person procedure-learning benchmarks.

  7. IEEE Access
    IEEE Access2024

    Toward Visual Syntactical Understanding

    Sayeed Shafayet Chowdhury, Soumyadeep Chandra, and Kaushik Roy

    IEEE Access

    arXivDOI

    An image can be viewed as a visual "sentence" whose semantic parts act as "words". This work shows that state-of-the-art CNNs and vision transformers fail to discriminate between syntactically correct and incorrect images (e.g., a face with eyes and nose swapped), and proposes an unsupervised three-stage framework — detecting the "words", masking and reconstructing them with a BERT-style masked autoencoder, and comparing original and reconstructed parts — to determine syntactic correctness, reaching 92.10% and 90.89% accuracy on CelebA and AFHQ.
